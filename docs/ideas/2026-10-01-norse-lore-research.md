# Norse lore research for veil goggles and hidden plants (2026-10-01)

Brainstorming input, not a spec. Items marked (?) are uncertain or from my own knowledge rather than a fetched source.

## 1. Invisibility lore

| Item | Source | Does | Who | Depiction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Huliðshjálmr ("helm of hiding") | Icelandic magic tradition; method varied (hands on the head, powders thrown) per [Wikipedia: Helm of Awe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helm_of_Awe) | Conceals the user | Magic users | A concept more than an object. It is not worn. |
| Ægishjálmr ("helm of awe") | Reginsmál prose: Fáfnir wears it and all living things fear it ([Fáfnir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A1fnir)) | Terror, not hiding. Often confused with the hiding helm. | Fáfnir the dragon | Eight-armed radial sigil |
| Tarnkappe / tarnhût ("concealing cloak/skin") | Nibelungenlied ([Cloak of invisibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloak_of_invisibility)) | Invisibility plus the strength of 12 men (the article says only "increases strength") | Dwarf Alberich, then Siegfried | Cloak or skin |
| Tarnhelm | Wagner's Ring ([Tarnhelm](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Tarnhelm)) | Invisibility and shapeshifting. Not in the original Andvari myth in Reginsmál. | Alberich and Mime | Helmet. Wagner's invention. |
| Hulinhjálmur ("helm of disguise") | Icelandic Galdrabók and similar grimoires, 17th c. ([Galdrastafir](https://galdrasyning.is/en/galdrastafir/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icelandic_magical_staves)) | A stave or sigil that makes the user unseen. Reportedly used in battle. | Whoever draws it | Radial or cross stave drawn on paper, skin or metal |
| Huldufólk / huldrefolk ("hidden folk") | Icelandic, Faroese and Norwegian folklore ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulduf%C3%B3lk)) | Live in boulders, cliffs and mounds. Invisible unless they choose to be seen. | Elf-like folk with grey clothes | The hulder is a forest woman ([Hulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulder)). She is often shown with a cow's tail (?). |

Not found: Hel hiding things, and Frigg/Freyja concealment mists. I did not find a good source, so treat them as unverified. Hel's realm is Niflheim and "helheim" means concealed/covered (?, folk etymology: *hel* is related to "to hide"). Useful hook: "hel" and "hehlen" come from the same root, "to conceal".

Design takeaways:
- "Helm" is the natural word: the three veil tiers could be named after a helm of hiding.
- Dwarves (Alberich) are the traditional makers. That fits Valheim's Dvergr in the Mistlands.
- Wagner's Tarnhelm is fiction. Use it for visuals only.

## 2. Lore for seeing the hidden

- **Heimdallr**: sees 100 leagues, needs less sleep than a bird, hears grass and wool grow ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heimdall)). Scholarly reading: he left an ear in Mímir's well, as Odin left an eye ([Mímisbrunnr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%ADmisbrunnr)).
- **Odin and Mímir**: Odin gave an eye for wisdom from the well. Eye-in-water is a strong image for a lens.
- **Fylgja / hamingja**: the animal fylgja is only seen by people with second sight, or in dreams. Sagas such as Njáls saga show seers glimpsing the animal spirits of enemies before battle ([Fylgja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fylgja), [Hamingja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamingja)). This is a good hook for "see hidden players".
- **Ofreskir**: the term for people who see spirits. I could not confirm it from a source (?).
- **Seiðr / völva**: seeing the hidden by trance. Hook: the staff-bearing seeress.

### Goggles names

1. **Heimdall's Lenses**: sees far and sees what is hidden. Easy to recognise.
2. **Mímir's Glass**: eye in the well, a water-lens look.
3. **Odin's Wager** or **Allfather's Eye**: sacrificed eye. Darker tone, fits the Ashlands tier.
4. **Seeress' Gaze** (Völva's Gaze): second sight, fits the player-reveal tier.
5. **Fylgja Spectacles**: shows the spirit-animal of hidden players. Best for the "hidden players" feature.

Alternative: one line of three, such as Heimdall's Lenses I/II/III, or three names: Watchman's Glass (Black Forest), Mímir's Glass (Mountains), Allfather's Eye (Ashlands).

## 3. Plants

Folklore found:
- Fern spores were thought to be "invisible fern seed" that gives invisibility ([Fern folklore](https://www.spirit-of-plants.com/fern-folklore/), [Fern flower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern_flower)). The strongest match for this mod.
- Mistletoe killed Baldr. It is a white-berried talisman against witchcraft ([Solar Blooms](https://thewickedgriffin.com/flowers-in-norse-mythology/)).
- St John's Wort is tied to Baldr, "Baldr's wounds" ([same source](https://thewickedgriffin.com/flowers-in-norse-mythology/)).
- Henbane is sacred to Thor, used in beer (same source). Angelica worn on the neck guards against witches ([Folkard, Plant Lore](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/44638/44638-h/44638-h.htm)).
- Fly agaric, bog myrtle and juniper: I did not source these (?). Bog myrtle (gale) was used in Norse beer (?). Juniper was burned for protection (?).

Valheim already has: Thistle, Dandelion, Cloudberry, Raspberry, Blueberry, Carrot, Turnip, Onion, Mushroom, Yellow Mushroom, Magecap, Jotun Puffs, Fiddlehead, Vineberry, Ashvine (Ashlands items per the [wiki](https://valheim.fandom.com/wiki/Fiddlehead)). Do not clone these names or looks. No vanilla Mountains plant that I know of (?).

### Plant concepts

1. **Black Forest: Huldra's Hair (Huldrelokk)**. A pale grey-green moss or lichen hanging from dead branches and standing stones. A small, faint cold shimmer. Grows near Greydwarf nests, old standing stones and tree stumps. "The hidden woman leaves her hair where she was last seen."
2. **Mountains: Baldr's Tear (Baldrsgrát)**. A small white-blue flower in snow, with a berry like mistletoe. About knee high, pulsing glow. Grows near Fenring caves and frozen ground. "Frigg asked everything to spare her son but passed over the small one."
3. **Ashlands: Hel's Ember Fern (Helfern)**. A black fern with red spore dust. Glows like embers, releases sparks. Grows in lava fields and around Charred ruins. "Fern seed hides the one who carries it. Down here it burns."

Optional alternative: Black Forest could be a henbane-like bloom (Thor's Henbane); the Mountains could use a yarrow-like cluster.

## 4. What Valheim has already

- Mistlands mist is cleared by the Wisplight (ID "Demister"), made of Silver and Wisp, 10 m radius ([Wisplight](https://valheim.weirdgloop.org/w/Wisplight)). Our goggles must not feel like a Demister clone: no mist-clearing, and a different slot (helmet or utility vs. the Wisplight).
- Hildir's quests: Geirrhafa (Black Forest, sealed tower), Brenna (Mountains), Plains cave ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/valheim-hildirs-quest-request-walkthrough/)). Her shelf is mostly cosmetics. Hook: sell goggle blueprints or cosmetics there? Optional.
- Ghosts exist in vanilla (Burial Chambers and Crypts). No vanilla "veil" or "hidden" lore that I found. So the namespace is free.
